Our ‘Nominate a Friend’ campaign is just a take on the old referral programme.  Up for grabs was a nice £250 electric guitar and amp.  Children had ONE week to nominate ONE friend to get ONE golden ticket to enter our prize draw.  We did a live stream event and drew the winning ticket for the lucky winner.  Simples.  Here’s how we did it.

**Phase 1 – what we did to prepare****…**

The idea was to create scarcity, time pressure & exclusivity
Give the parents a ‘heads up’ WhatsApp message to say ‘this is just a bit of fun, no pressure to participate’.  Let the kids do the selling.
We ‘toured’ the prize around all of our group locations to every single group to create a buzz with our head Tutor playing some wicked licks, distortion, the works - this was time heavy, but totally worth it.  The kids left their lessons waving the forms in the parents faces.
We had a solid coms strategy to parents:
Initial welcome call
Followed up by a WhatsApp message with free trial details
Reminder WhatsApp text ‘hope you’re excited about your lesson’,
Final ‘were looking forward to meeting you today’ WhatsApp message
A final call to anybody who not engaging
We said to parents/ kids they had only 1 week to hand back forms, but we actually gave them 2 weeks to return the forms.

**Phase 2 - What we did when it ****actually kicked**** off**

Made a phone call to each new parent to introduce our club and invite to free 30 min 1/2/1 with Crawford.  We tended to do this in the late afternoon after 3pm school run or evening.
We included a link to our online brochure and end of year concert brochures so nobody turned up saying ‘how much does this cost’?
We asked parents if we needed to make any adjustments for their children
We personalised all of our whatsap messages and sent them mostly to THE CHILD.  So… ‘Hey Johnny, we hope you’re looking forward to your lesson’.  If you send it addressing the child, the mum will show them and creates better engagement.
We collected lots of ‘IRRELEVANT’ information on welcome calls like, Johnny loves to play football or Abbey likes to go horse riding.  We then used this info at the trial lesson, so the parents and kids felt like we already knew them.

**Phase ****3  -**** ****The ****trial lesson **** **

Asked our office manager to attend free trials on site to meet & greet parents at the door by name
We allocated 45 mins per 1-2-1 trial lesson so as parents/ kids not to feel rushed
We held these lessons on the same days and times as other group workshops were taking place so they could feel the buzz and see our groups in action. We invited new students to ‘sit in’ on groups after their free trial.  We prepped groups tutors to be super engaging.
We Insisted that parents sit in on the free trial lesson regardless of age & experience.  This was very effective.
We had club info already prepared for the parents to read during free trial lesson, club concert/ brochure etc.
All new students learned the same thing, ‘we will rock you’, 3 x 1 finger chords with a mute.
Children went home with a certificate & cheat sheet with chord boxes.
Parents received a ‘tutorial video’ of we will rock you sent to their whatsap.
Children and parents were offered to take a loan guitar home there and then!

**Phase 4 - Mopping up**

We phoned everybody who had a trial lesson
Children went home with a certificate & cheat sheet with chord boxes.
Parents received a ‘tutorial video’ of we will rock you sent to their whatsap.

**The results**
We had 50 leads
We booked 48 children into 1-2-1 lots over 2 weeks
We converted 43 of those children into customers
The competition generated a ‘buzz’ locally so we had an additional 13 students get in touch
We slotted them in and converted 8 of those to students
Total 51 students

**Big take aways**
Get the RIGHT PEOPLE doing both the trial lesson and the administration
This is a LOT of work!
Do not let a single lead get away without a firm ‘NO WE DON’T WANT LESSONS’
Make sure you thank and reward everyone who took part, including the parents
Buy a small ‘gift’ for just taking part (we got branded plectrums)

Rince and repeat!
