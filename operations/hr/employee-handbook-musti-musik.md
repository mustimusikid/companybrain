### 1: Musti Musik Mission

### 2: Customer Obsession

Customer is at the heart of everything I do. I don't just serve customers; I ask their needs, exceed their expectations, and constantly innovate to improve their experience.

This means putting myself in our customers' shoes with every decision I make. I ask myself: "How does this benefit the customer?" If it doesn't, I go back to the drawing board. I'm not afraid to sacrifice short-term gains for long-term customer satisfaction because I know that happy customers are the key to our success. Let this be your guide to every decision you make.

Customer obsession also means actively seeking and valuing customer feedback. I listen to what our customers say, both the good and the bad. I see complaints as opportunities for improvement and praise as validation of our efforts. And you should too.

Our customers are aspiring jazz and gospel musicians, passionate learners, and individuals who've placed their trust in us to guide their musical journey. You and I have a responsibility to honor that trust by consistently delivering value and exceeding expectations.

### 3: Be Yourself

I believe that to have a fulfilling life is to live true to yourself. Live in authenticity and passion. I have reached this point in my life because I stayed true to myself. I believe that in order to be great, you need to be yourself. Thus, I also want you to be yourself, with all your quirks, strengths, and even weaknesses. Why? Because it's only when you're truly yourself that you can tap into your full potential.

Being yourself doesn’t mean embracing your weaknesses, it doesn’t mean freely disrespecting or harassing someone (if you do then you shouldn’t be here). What I mean by being yourself is to not work in something you don’t truly enjoy. If you love video editing and you’re our video editor, then you’re gonna have fun while doing your work. If you hate tech but you work full time at tech, you’re in the wrong position.

It also doesn’t mean that you need to enjoy every bit of your work. For example, myself. The CEO needs to learn every part of the business. I hate tech. I hate customer success. But that’s what I need to do in order to fulfill my true calling, which is Musti Musik Mission. That’s why I wake up everyday not feeling dreaded to fulfill the things I don’t like.

That’s the 1st requirement. The 2nd that I see so many people is that they don’t go all in. In order for you to fulfill the 2nd requirement you need the 1st. Now let me explain the 2nd part.

Going "all in" means more than just working hard. It means throwing yourself fully into your work, bringing your unique perspective, creativity, and energy to everything you do. It means staying longer hours because you know every work given is an opportunity to improve yourself. I’m not looking for people who just want a job that they like; I’m looking for those who see their work as a calling, a vital part of who they are.

You can’t fake going all in. I know when you don’t. Some symptoms:
Using ChatGPT solely for your work. Someone did and they don’t work here anymore.
Watching the clock and rushing out the door at the end of the working hour.
Only doing the bare minimum required for each task to check in the worklist.
Avoiding challenges or difficult projects.

This isn't about burning out or sacrificing your personal life. It's about finding that sweet spot where your work doesn't feel like work because you're so passionate about it. I believe that when you love what you do, it shows in the quality of your work and the impact you make.

### 4: You Are An Owner, Not A Renter

I see a LOT of people don’t care about their work. That’s simply a sad way of living. You spend around 90,000 hours of your life at work. That’s ⅓ of your life dedicated to work. How could someone not care about doing something they spend ⅓ of their life?

Actually there’s a few reasons why. The biggest reason is bureaucracy (even the word sounds horrible). Bureaucracy stifles creativity and fosters hopelessness. Why bother making something great when it doesn’t push the needle forward? Why bother when the guy who’s not doing anything but smart in talking gets promoted while you dont?
That’s why what I can promise to you is that in Musti Musik, every act matters. Every work that you do here realizes the mission. I promise you that but **you need to care**.

But what is considered as caring? Like an owner to anything you deem precious, like an owner to your home, an owner to your life, you make sure that those things are treated with respect, attention, and dedication. Caring about your work means:
Taking pride in what you do, no matter how small the task.
Constantly seeking ways to improve and innovate.
Being proactive in solving problems, not just pointing them out.
Going the extra mile when needed, not because you have to, but because you want to.

When you care, you don't just clock in and out. You invest yourself. You bring your whole self to work. You think critically about how to make things better. You take ownership of your projects and see them through to success.

### 5: Tell It Like It Is

Telling the truth, even when it's difficult, is key to our trust-based culture. I believe in radical candor - caring personally while challenging directly. This means I give honest feedback continuously, I admit my mistakes, and I'm not afraid to disagree respectfully. I know that constructive conflict leads to better outcomes.

I also believe in disagreeing to agree. I believe that fully formed ideas are made through stark disagreements and opinions rather than simply agreeing to your boss’ demands.

### 6: Trust Each Other

Trust is everything. You trust that your partner doesn't cheat. You trust that your friends don’t stab you in the back. You trust that your God is protecting you. You trust that your parents love you. You trust that your company is loyal to you. Trust is everything.

That’s why I want to make a trust-based culture. It means believing that your colleagues will do their jobs. It means creating an environment where everyone feels safe to be vulnerable, take risks, and speak their minds.

When I say "we don't fuck each other up," I mean we have each other's backs. I don't throw colleagues under the bus, engage in office politics, or undermine each other's efforts. Instead, I support one another, celebrate each other's successes, and step up to help when someone is struggling.

We also depend on each other. When I commit to something, I follow through. I meet deadlines, I deliver on promises, and I communicate proactively if I encounter obstacles. This reliability builds trust and allows us to work efficiently as a cohesive unit.

### 7: Best Idea Wins

There’s no bureaucracy in Musti Musik. I value people for their merit, for their value, and for their impact.

This doesn't mean being disrespectful. It means having the courage to speak up, challenge the status quo, and propose better solutions, regardless of your position. I believe that the best ideas can come from anywhere, and I want to create an environment where those ideas can flourish.

This means that if you're the newest intern and you have a groundbreaking idea, I want to hear it. If you see a flaw in a senior member's proposal, I expect you to point it out constructively. This approach ensures that we're always operating at our best, driven by merit rather than hierarchy.

### 8: Let Builders Build

I believe in empowering our team members to turn their visions into reality. When I say "Let Builders Build," I mean providing the resources, support, and autonomy needed for creative minds to flourish.

If you have an idea that aligns with our mission, I want to give you the space to develop it. This might mean allocating time, budget, or other resources to your project. It also means removing bureaucratic obstacles that might hinder your progress.

My role is to create an environment where builders can thrive. I trust in your expertise and creativity, and I'm here to support you in bringing your ideas to life.

### 9: Work In Days, Not Months

What separates startups from big corporations is how fast we make decisions, and in the rapidly evolving world of music education, speed is key. I want swift action over endless deliberation.

And it’s okay to be wrong! Though I'm not advocating for recklessness, it means being willing to launch products or initiatives that aren't perfect. If a bold move risks some disruption but has the potential for impact, that's a risk I'm willing to take.

That’s the mindset you need to have. I'd rather see ten bold attempts with seven failures and three successes than ten safe moves that lead to mediocre results.

### 10: Bad News Is Good News

In any company, problems arise daily. What sets us apart at Musti Musik is how we handle problems. When I say "Bad News Is Good News," I'm promoting a culture of transparency.

I am genuinely happy when you share bad news. Here's why:
Early Detection: A small issue today could become a catastrophe tomorrow. We tackle them while they're still manageable.
Trust Building: When you share bad news, it shows that you trust me and the team enough to be vulnerable. Go back to culture code no. 5.
Learning Opportunities: Every piece of bad news is a chance to learn and improve our processes. It's not about pointing fingers; it's about finding solutions and preventing similar issues in the future.

While I love bad news, I have a problem when the same bad news are repeated:
First Time: When you make a mistake and bring it to my attention quickly, I'll be grateful. We'll work together to fix it and put measures in place to prevent it from happening again.
Second Time: I'll be concerned. It means looking at our preventive measures again or providing additional training.
Third Time: It's a serious issue. This shows a lack of learning.

Repeated mistakes happen either as a systemic problem that we need to address at an organizational level, or an individual performance issue. Neither of these is acceptable in the long term.

By this principle, I am creating a tough organization that delves in bad news all the time and solves it all the time. We become tough.

## Human Resources Policy

The purpose of Human Resources is to help you thrive here. HR have a bad rep in big corporations for executing layoffs and dirty practices etc but people forget that their core purpose is to help you enjoy your work. HR helps Musti Musik become a good company to work for.

Thus there’s a few policies that HR set for you to enjoy your work. Made by my HR guy.

### 1: Attendance
Employees are expected to work Monday to Friday, flexible in time. **Attendance is recorded through the Worklist**—failure to check in will be considered an absence. **Unexcused lateness or absence** results in prorated salary deductions. If an employee is absent for more than **five consecutive days without notice**, they will be considered to have resigned.

### 2: Leave
Each employee is entitled to **one day of leave per month** for personal reasons. Leave requests must be submitted to HR **at least one day in advance**, except for emergencies. For sick leave exceeding one day, a **doctor’s note** is required. **Bereavement leave** is granted as follows: up to **5 days** for immediate family, and **1-2 days** for other relatives. Non-urgent leave must be compensated with **overtime or weekend work**, as agreed with the Head. Failure to follow these procedures may result in a warning.

### 3: Resignation
Employees wishing to resign must submit a resignation letter to HR **at least two weeks** before the intended date, stating a clear reason for their departure. They are responsible for completing a **job handover** and may be asked to assist in finding a replacement. The handover process, including task transfer and access handover, should be completed at least **one week before** the last working day to ensure a smooth transition.

### 4: Worklist and Night Report
Employees must review their **Worklist** daily to check tasks that need to be done. **Tasks completed** should be checked off in the Worklist, and any unfinished tasks must be explained in the Night Report. Night Reports must be submitted **by the end of the day max**. **Late submissions** require a valid explanation; failure to provide one may result in disciplinary action. If an employee fails to complete these actions three times, they may receive a warning.

### 5: Termination (PHK)
Termination requires **three prior warnings** unless for serious violations. The company will provide **written notice at least two weeks in advance** of the effective termination date. Employees must **complete all pending tasks** and **handover job-related access** by the termination date. Grounds for termination include: **extended illness** (more than two weeks), **serious misconduct** (e.g., criminal acts), **poor performance**, and **ethical violations** (e.g., conflict of interest).

### 6: Disciplinary Action (Surat Peringatan)
Employees may receive warnings for violations such as **dishonesty, negligence, poor performance**, or **behavior** that negatively impacts the company. HR and the Head will **identify and document violations**, and HR will issue warnings via email, confirmed through WhatsApp. The employee has **one week** to correct their behavior; otherwise, further warnings may be issued. **Warnings are valid for six months**, after which they expire if no further violations occur.

Our small size means every person counts. You count and you matter. We all depend on you. Every initiative you make, you can see a customer/employee gets happy because of your idea. So if you want to do work that matters and people depend on you, you’re in the best place to do it.

### Compensation Philosophy

“In large part, culture is a product of compensation.” – Alec Haverstick. This quote changed my life on how I approach compensation. I had wondered why the culture code I made was not materializing the way I wanted it to be, but now I know, I had no formal compensation philosophy! I paid people what I thought was fair and increased salaries based on my own judgement. There had been no written process!

So, after careful consideration, I present to you our compensation philosophy. Note that this will evolve as our company grows, but what you are reading now is what I truly believe to be fitting for our size now.

**What is the role of compensation in Musti Musik?**

We view compensation as the set of tools that **enforce our culture and attract the right kind of people to thrive here**, which can be seen in Chapter 5. Thus, we don’t expect people to work harder because we pay them more money. But we believe that our incentives and non monetary gains such as mentorship and learning experiences attract the right kind of people and make them take decisions and set priorities that benefit Musti Musik in the long term.

Again, we are obsessed with our customers and their successes. By ensuring we attract the right team (those who are themselves here and are fulfilled with their work), we trust that this will trickle down to customers as well.

These key principles guide all the decisions we make about compensation and benefits:
Transparency: We share our approach here and all salaries to create trust, hold ourselves accountable.
Culture-obsessed: We value our culture, thus our compensation and benefits reflect what we truly believe and attract those who fit our culture. Therefore, a variety of compensation schemes are employed depending on departments.
3:2 Rule: We believe 1 great person ≥ 3 good people (especially for creatives which can easily be 15x), thus it is beneficial for us to have three times the productivity at two times the payroll cost.
Fairness: We ensure that those with the same role, responsibilities, and experience level are paid equitably.

**What is our pay positioning relative to the market?**

We establish base salaries at the 50th percentile of the intern market. For total compensation, we aspire to be at the 95th percentile. Together with our brand, our culture, and our benefits, our total rewards package should position us as a top three employer in our industry.

**Who decides what and when around compensation? How do you deal with compensation in the budgeting process?**

Our CEO is ultimately accountable for everything concerned with talent and compensation. Decisions about the compensation system are generally taken by the cofounders team. Decisions regarding the compensation of individuals within the given system are made by a compensation committee (consisting of Cofounders, Finance, and HR), based on the suggestion of your division head. Salaries are analyzed once a month as part of the budgeting process. The CEO leads the compensation committee.

**What pay components do we offer, and what is the proportion between them?**

Base salary is determined by a pay structure with pay grades. The spread between the minimum and maximum of each grade is wide, and grades have significant overlap between them. This allows us to reward outstanding performance within the same pay grade. It can also lead to situations where a team member has a higher compensation than his/her manager.

In addition to a base salary, we offer individual incentives and group gain-sharing programs for a few customer-facing departments. Bonuses are distributed equally among the department to encourage teamwork.

For the majority of our team members, the base salary represents 75 percent or more of total compensation.

Salespeople receive 100 percent of their total compensation as variable components. Variable pay is contingent on results. Without results, there will be no variable pay.

Employees can increase their compensation through (1) promotion or (2) increase in pay grades (until it is capped), both based on individual and team performance. Performance is graded every month through our performance development review (PDR) and our monthly budgeting for human resources.
