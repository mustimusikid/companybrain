**Mo Li Hua**
Mo Li Hua is an 18th century Chinese folk song from the Qing dynasty. This song is famous for its beautiful melody and the impact caused by this specific folk song. Mo Li Hua also known as “jasmine song” has two different versions, one version about giving jasmine flowers and the other version expresses the fear of plucking those jasmine flowers.
The song “Mo Li Hua” has some stories behind it and is very impactful in China. Therefore in this essay, I would like to explain some of those reasons below.
Firstly, based on the title itself it means the flower Jasmine. Jasmine flowers could represent grace, elegance, and purity. This also can be learned from the lyrics that express love and beauty. The beautiful and peaceful melody, also the song's lyrics create an emotional feeling for those who listen.
Secondly, this song was used for multiple events such as 2004 Summer Olympics, 2008 Summer Olympics and 2010 Shanghai Expo opening ceremony. But, there was an important event that happened in China in 2011 that involved this song which was the reason why this song is now banned in that country. Those events were pro-democracy / peaceful protests also known as the “*Greater Chinese Democratic Jasmine Revolution*” which was inspired after the Jasmine Revolution in Tunisia. In February 2011, microbloggers passed around tweets calling for protests at 2 PM on February 20, 2011 in a dozen major Chinese cities, including Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou, which despite being blocked is still widely used by activists in China. Participants were urged to conduct a ‘strolling’ protest against the government and shout slogans like “*We want food, we want work, we want housing, we want fairness*.”, *“We want equality, we want justice”*,* “Start political reform, end the one-party dictatorship or stop the one-party dictatorship”*, *“Lift the restrictions of the press, press freedom”, “Freedom for **, democracy for ten thousand years” *, some posts urged participants to stroll silently holding a jasmine flower. A post signed "*The Initiators of Chinese Jasmine Revolution*" read: "*No shouting or slogans, just walking and smiling*.". In this peaceful protest, activists used Mo Li Hua by playing this song on their phones and throwing jasmine petals while strolling around the streets to express dissent against the government.
Due to the protest and its connection to jasmine flowers, the government of China did extreme things in response to this protest. For example, the song Mo Li Hua was banned / blocked from China, including the internet and all social media. Mo Li Hua was heavily censored in Chinese websites and social media by removing all video posts of this song and preventing online searchers for this song. Not just that, many protesters were taken into custody for throwing a jasmine flower during the protest or placed under house arrest to prevent the planned demonstrations. The authorities sent a large number of police in some cities like Beijing and Shanghai to monitor and disperse any gatherings and also to identify potential protesters.
**Chick Chick**
**	**Chick Chick is a song from Wang Rong, a Chinese singer. During her career she was one of the biggest female artists in mainland China around the year 2005 before she chose to be a dance pop artist. After 2009, she departed from her former music label and decided to start her own company. Sadly, her company did not do well commercially,  that’s why she started doing a “*social **experment*” by making catchy and more entertaining music such as “Chick Chick”.
	Chick Chick was released in October 2014 and quickly gained millions of views on website platforms, such as YouTube. This amount of views made this song “*The next Gangnam Style*” which was one of the most popular and most watched music videos from South Korea.
	The inspiration behind this song was based on a vivid dream she had. One time, she dreamed about animal conversation. In that dream, she could understand the animals’ chatter despite it being nonsensical. And when she woke up after that dream, she decided to transform her weird, unusual, and bizarre dream into a song.
	At first, she was going to write the lyrics with the dialogue between the animals, but that did not mesh well with the music. Eventually, she opted for the repetitive phrase “cluck cluck day” which is the sound that chickens make. Her decision by using literal chicken  sounds for the lyrics, made this song sound more playful and gave the listeners an experience to imagine what the animals might be saying, also the song sound more catchy and entertaining which was aligned with her motto and goal in creating catchy and entertaining music.
	This song successfully became the potential Chinese pop music to achieve international recognition through viral marketing tactics. As I said before about Wang Rong and her social experiment in making music, this was one of the examples and she succeeded. This type of song genre is considered mainstream in China, because people in China are generally conservative about values and behaviors while this song sounds bizarre and the music video is very weird. But, despite all the bizarreness, this song became successful with millions of views and inspired other Chinese singers to experiment and explore more diverse music genres.
**This Is China**
**	**The last song I want to use as an example of songs that changed China is titled “This Is China”. This Is China is a rap song by Chinese rap group called CD Rev featuring rapper Chukkie. CD Rev who is also known as “*Chengdu Revolution*” is different from any other rap groups. CD Rev are using rap to challenge the world's media over its portrayal of China. Their songs are often themed about patriotism, social critique, and also cultural identity. One of their most famous songs is This Is China.
	This Is China was released in June 2016. This song’s lyrics showed criticism about how Western media stereotypes China despite all the achievements in China. This made the CD Rev music genre a blend of hip hop with state propaganda. In the making of This Is China, The Communist Youth League was really involved in many things such as helping with the song production by sponsoring the studio to record the music video and also influenced the patriotic themes and messaging in the song.
	Basically, this song was aimed to promote a positive image of China to Western media stereotypes. This Is China successfully became a creative form of online patriotism that helped amplify nationalist sentiments and messaging to a wider audience and became international attention by raising the profile of China’s online nationalist movement globally.
